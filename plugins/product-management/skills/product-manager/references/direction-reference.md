# From purpose to a learning roadmap

Use these as connected questions, not five compulsory documents. Strategy informs objectives; outcomes from delivery and discovery can change the strategy.

All teacher-product examples below are fictional teaching examples, not validated recommendations.

| Layer | Question | Useful output | Connected example | Common mistake | Suite route |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mission / purpose | Why do we exist, and for whom? | A useful purpose that guides decisions; combine with vision if separate statements add no value. | Help independent teachers prepare useful lessons without losing evenings. | A slogan with no decision relevance. | pm-vision |
| Vision / winning aspiration | What worthwhile future are we pursuing? | A motivating desired future and what success means; revisit with strategic choices. | Teachers can adapt a lesson confidently within their available planning time. | A revenue target presented as the entire customer-facing purpose. | pm-vision |
| Strategy | Where will we play, how will we win, and what will we decline? | Connected audience, value, cost, capability, distribution and trade-off choices, with assumptions. | Focus on solo language teachers; offer editable, vetted lesson patterns; decline a full school-management system. | A feature list, universal superiority claim or untested claim competitors cannot copy. | pm-strategy |
| Objectives + key results | What meaningful outcome should change in this period, and how will we know? | A significant problem, owner, baseline, target rationale, horizon and guardrails. | Help first-time teachers reach a usable lesson sooner. Measure first-success rate and time to a saved, usable plan; establish unknown baselines before setting targets. | Counting shipped templates as proof that teachers obtained value. | pm-objectives |
| Roadmap | Which bets and learning come first, and why? | Outcome-linked sequencing, dependencies, confidence, review criteria and explicit commitment status. | Now: discover first-lesson friction. Next: test the smallest promising intervention. Later: expand only if outcome evidence supports it. | A fixed calendar of features with no link to objectives. | pm-roadmap |

## Metrics across the layers
A North Star is a proposed indicator of customer value, not a substitute for strategy. Input metrics describe plausible drivers; verify the relationship. Key results select relevant measurable changes for the current objective. Business measures describe value captured, and guardrails protect quality and other important outcomes. A team needs meaningful influence over its measures; this does not guarantee causal control.

## Teaching route
Start with the current decision. Explain its layer using the example, draft the user’s equivalent from available context, show the link to the adjacent layer, and resolve one consequential question. If the chain breaks, investigate the missing rationale rather than fill every box with confident language.

## Source and interpretation
Original suite synthesis informed by Paweł Huryn, Product Vision vs Strategy vs Objectives vs Roadmap: The Advanced Edition, January 6, 2024. Source originals are not included; use the bundled guidance and Markdown worksheets. No article figures reproduced. The source prefers merging mission/vision and small metric sets; those are options, not mandatory definitions or quotas. Winning aspiration can sit within strategy. Source targets are examples, not benchmarks. A strong strategy is a hypothesis about coherent choices, not guaranteed defensibility.
