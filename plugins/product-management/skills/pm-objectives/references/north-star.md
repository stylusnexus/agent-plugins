# North Star: glossary, selection and use

Source: Paweł Huryn, The North Star Framework 101, July 1, 2023. Company examples are the article's claims, not verified current metrics. Source originals are not included.

## Glossary
- North Star metric: the focal indicator proposed to represent customer value for a scoped product. Its link to durable business/project success is a hypothesis to evaluate.
- Input metric: an actionable measure believed to influence that value; document the mechanism and uncertainty.
- Constellation: the North Star and its connected input metrics. Add levels only when they clarify team influence, not to create a decorative hierarchy.
- OMTM (One Metric That Matters): the immediate focal measure for a stage or constraint; it may differ from the longer-lived North Star.
- KPI: an important indicator being monitored; not every KPI represents customer value.
- Key result: a time-bound measured change under an objective; it can use an input metric or North Star where meaningful influence exists.
- Business measure: value captured or viability, such as revenue; pair it with customer outcomes.
- Guardrail: a measure with explicit intervention criteria to prevent harmful optimization.

## Selection workflow
1. Identify the customer/segment, promised value and natural use cadence. Consider attention, transactions or productivity as lenses, not exhaustive product categories. Personal/community tools need not optimize commercial growth or habitual use.
2. Draft a small candidate set and compare closeness to achieved value, clarity, measurement feasibility, influence, gaming risk and important exclusions. Do not assume time spent or activity volume is beneficial.
3. Select a provisional focal metric with rationale and alternatives. Define qualifying value event, population, unit, window, formula, data source, owner, exclusions and unknown baseline. One per coherent product/value model is a focus heuristic; do not force unrelated businesses into a meaningless aggregate.
4. Map a manageable set of inputs with labelled hypothesized mechanisms. Connect to retention, sustainability or another relevant longer-term outcome. Investigate confounding and selection bias; correlation or an interview alone does not establish causality.
5. Pair with guardrails and review triggers. Use real observations to revisit proxy quality. Source example percentages and causal-looking relationships are teaching hypotheses, not targets or benchmarks.

## Where to use it
Strategy/canvas: articulate value and key assumptions. Objectives: choose a controllable outcome to improve now. Growth: diagnose which input constrains valuable use. Roadmap: connect bets to the intended change. Discovery: test whether the metric represents value. Do not replace strategy with a number or manufacture a North Star when value/data are still unclear.

## Output and tools
