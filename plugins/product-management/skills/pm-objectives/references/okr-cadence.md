# OKRs as a learning and decision cycle

Informed by Paweł Huryn, *Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) 101*, August 19, 2023. The article primarily interprets Christina Wodtke's approach; it is not a claim to have read Radical Focus or an authoritative specification for all OKR practices.

## Start
Connect an objective to a meaningful problem and the strategy. Agree on ownership, decision latitude, dependencies, review horizon and guardrails. Choose a small enough set to focus attention; one objective is a useful starting option, not a universal quota. Co-create key results with the people doing the work.

Define each result's metric, population/cohort, baseline/date, target/rationale, deadline, source and owner. Missing baselines remain unknown; propose measuring them or label a provisional target explicitly. Do not present guessed targets or a 50% success probability as evidence. Distinguish aspirational targets from actual commitments.

## During the cycle
Use an existing useful cadence rather than duplicating meetings. Review current value versus baseline/target, data freshness and quality, confidence with rationale, health/guardrail metrics, learnings, blockers, and the next decision. Confidence is judgment, not completion percentage. Integrate discovery and delivery bets; activity completion is not automatically a key result.

Define an explicit response for a breached guardrail based on severity and exposure. Do not mechanically stop all work for any red indicator. Record why a target, scope or approach changes; never quietly move the goalposts.

## Close and learn
Compare outcomes with the starting position, explain uncertainty and unintended effects, and decide continue/adapt/stop. Separate contribution from causal proof. Review the strategy if the objective no longer serves it. Decide next-cycle priorities using new evidence rather than rolling everything forward automatically.

## Adapt to context
Align team outcomes with organizational direction without mechanically cascading tasks. Preserve actual team dependencies rather than assuming independence. Use a hypothesis/learning objective when the critical task is reducing uncertainty; specify the evidence and decision it should enable, not just a count of interviews. Operational health remains important even outside the focal objective.

Do not require OKRs for every person or project, use them as surveillance, or equate missed stretch targets with individual performance. Personal learning goals are optional and distinct from employee evaluation. Adapt to Scrum or other workflows only when used by the team; the article's preferred Scrum mapping is not the only valid approach.

Critique examples: “build a landing page/mobile app” is an output even if called inspiring; automated ticket resolution can harm outcomes if customers remain stuck. Pair throughput/efficiency measures with customer success and quality. Source examples and targets are illustrative, not benchmarks. North Star metrics also require evidence that they represent value; naming one does not guarantee business success.

## Optional check-in artifact
Objective and strategy link; key result baseline/current/target/source/date; guardrail status; evidence and confidence; changed assumptions; next action/owner; next review. Generate only as much structure as the project needs.
