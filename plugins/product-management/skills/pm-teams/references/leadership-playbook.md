# Product leadership playbook

Original suite adaptation informed by Paweł Huryn, The Product Leadership Playbook, June 17, 2023. No full reading of the books it cites is claimed.

Use the relevant play for a real situation. For a solo builder, translate team practices into clear decisions, collaborator agreements and reflection; do not simulate an organization that does not exist.

## 1. Establish context before delegating
State the customer problem, vision, strategic choices, objective, constraints, evidence and decision rights. Agree what the collaborator owns, when to seek help and how outcomes will be reviewed. Produce a concise context brief. Autonomy does not mean absent support.

## 2. Coach through unfamiliar work
Ask what is difficult, listen to an actual example, explain a useful concept and draft a next step together. Agree a small practice opportunity and follow-up. Adjust support to task familiarity and consequence with the person; do not diagnose a fixed maturity level from a job title or vocabulary. Use the chief's optional competency coaching map.

## 3. Work through disagreement
Describe the decision and shared outcome; separate observations, interpretations and preferences. Invite dissent, summarize each view fairly, compare trade-offs and decide within established authority. Record rationale, unresolved risks and revisit triggers. Psychological safety permits respectful challenge; it does not mean unanimous agreement or avoiding accountability.

## 4. Delegate with boundaries and support
Agree meaningful ownership, resources, dependencies, guardrails and a useful check-in cadence. Avoid prescribing implementation details merely to demonstrate control. Keep expertise boundaries explicit; a PM does not automatically have staffing or disciplinary authority over engineering/design.

## 5. Respond to setbacks
Stabilize urgent harm through the existing incident process; then examine evidence, decisions, conditions and learning without inventing motives or assigning blame. Separate acceptable experiment uncertainty from ignored commitments. Make one concrete improvement and recognize contributions accurately.

## 6. Decide under uncertainty
Compare options and the cost of delay, make reversible choices when justified, identify evidence that would change the decision and communicate the rationale. Being decisive is compatible with revising a choice. Do not treat confidence, calm presentation or charisma as evidence of correctness.

## 7. Maintain leadership habits
At an appropriate cadence ask: Is the purpose clear? Can people influence the work? Can concerns be raised? Is support available to learn? Adapt meetings to the team; do not create rituals for their own sake. Recognize progress, share credit and avoid promises about universal motivation or productivity.

Outputs may be a context brief, coaching plan, decision record, facilitation outline or retrospective. Draft messages locally; do not send them or conduct personnel actions without explicit authorization. The article's leader/manager distinctions and personal observations are guidance, not universal role definitions or guarantees.
