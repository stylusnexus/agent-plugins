# Initiative brief / living PRD

Use only sections that help the decision. Draft with product, design and engineering perspectives; record unknown collaborators rather than inventing them.

- **Summary/status:** problem, proposed approach and current decision in 2–3 sentences; draft/accepted status and date.
- **People:** owner, collaborators, decision roles and existing communication links when known.
- **Context:** why now, evidence and relevant changed conditions.
- **Strategic objective:** linked strategy, customer/company outcome, baseline/target/time horizon and measurement source; mark proposals and unknowns.
- **Audience:** segment, job, situation and relevant constraints.
- **Value:** before → enabling mechanism → desired outcome; alternatives and evidence links.
- **Options and scope:** recommendation, rejected alternatives, non-goals and smallest useful slice. Map proposed features to outcomes.
- **Experience:** key flows, prototypes/diagrams and necessary acceptance criteria; link detailed specifications rather than duplicating them.
- **Assumptions/risks:** evidence, tests, accepted residual risks, owner and revisit trigger. Include business model and go-to-market dependencies when relevant.
- **Release and learning:** estimate/horizon or explicitly agreed commitment, dependencies, rollout, monitoring/rollback, success review and remaining work.
- **References/change trail:** research, decisions, detailed implementation and launch/support materials; use the project's existing sources of truth.

Keep concise; omit irrelevant sections. A PRD supports collaboration and decisions, does not constitute approval to build or publish, and does not replace necessary acceptance criteria or technical documentation.
