# Teach strategy with examples and critique

Source: Paweł Huryn, *Product Strategy Examples*, October 26, 2024. Source originals are not included; use the bundled guidance and Markdown worksheets. The article presents illustrative Google Maps, Netflix and OpenAI strategies. They are not authenticated internal strategies, current product documentation or validated competitive analysis.

## Use examples to reveal connections
Choose one relevant example and explain a relationship, not an entire company profile. Then ask how that relationship differs in the user's product and draft their version together. Make an exercise optional; do not delay the requested work until the user passes a quiz.

| Article illustration | Useful teaching question | Critical check |
|---|---|---|
| Maps: navigation, mapping information and contributions | How might a capability improve value and encourage contributions that improve the capability? | Is the reinforcing loop supported by evidence? Which participant benefits and why would they contribute? |
| Streaming: distinctive content and localization | How do audience choices shape capabilities, investment and sacrifices? | Are content rights, economics and substitution barriers known? Unique content alone does not prove a defensible business. |
| AI: model capabilities, infrastructure and distribution | How do relative cost and differentiated value interact across segments or offerings? | Do stated trade-offs hold for the scoped product and date? Broad ethical or superiority claims need concrete evidence. |

These are interpretations of the author's examples, not assertions about current company behavior. Verify fresh primary evidence before using any company claim in real competitive analysis.

## Critique rather than copy
- Check each proposed metric against customer value. More time in a navigation product could mean friction rather than success; do not assume engagement duration should increase.
- Example growth/churn/usage targets are illustrative numbers, not benchmarks to transplant into the user's objectives. Require baseline, cohort, measurement definition and rationale.
- Inspect whether a “trade-off” is a deliberate reinforcing choice, a temporary omission, an unverified claim, or a constraint. The article's Netflix live-programming and OpenAI model/customization/hardware exclusions require dated primary verification before reuse. Do not present them as timeless facts.
- Separate inability to imitate from an incentive not to imitate. Investment, scale or exclusive assets may create barriers, but “impossible to copy” is not established by an attractive narrative.
- Where several segments are listed, avoid blending their value propositions. Choose the segment relevant to the decision and state cross-segment dependencies.

## Choose a representation by the question
The article suggests business/startup canvases for new products and its strategy canvas for existing ones. Treat that as a heuristic: use a business-model canvas when economics and participants are unclear, a lean/startup representation when testing foundational assumptions, and a strategy canvas when integrating choices. Product age alone does not determine the right tool. Avoid requiring several overlapping canvases.

## A useful teaching output
One relationship from an attributed example → one limitation/counterexample → a provisional equivalent for the user's product → evidence needed → next decision. Reuse the user's accepted choices. A polished example should make judgment easier, not replace it.
