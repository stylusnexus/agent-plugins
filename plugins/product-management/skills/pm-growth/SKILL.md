# Product Growth

Read [shared principles](../product-manager/references/principles.md) before applying this workflow independently. Use only the sections needed for the current decision.

## Workflow
1. Establish what growth means for this product and its mission: sustained valuable use, revenue, adoption, contributions, reach, or another appropriate outcome. Do not impose commercial scale on a personal or community tool.
2. Diagnose the current constraint with evidence across acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and monetization where applicable. Define each event and cohort. For a new product, label the proposed funnel and constraint as hypotheses.
3. Connect growth to where to play, how to win, and the value proposition. Identify the intended segment, first useful experience, recurring reason to return, buying/adoption process, and plausible distribution channels.
4. Compare a few relevant motions such as product-led adoption, community, content, partnerships, or sales-led outreach. Explain the fit, cost/capability demands, time to learn, and trade-offs. Do not recommend every channel or treat a tool list as a strategy.
For each candidate marketing channel (for example search/content, social/community, partnerships/resellers, email, paid acquisition, or direct sales), state segment access, intent, message/offer, conversion path, ownership, cost/capacity, time to signal, measurement, and dependency on a platform or partner. Choose a small testable mix, explain rejected channels, and distinguish channel from growth motion and growth loop. Verify current channel economics or platform constraints before relying on them. For a step-by-step playbook with named channels for one audience, move to that dedicated playbook work (internally `audience-playbook`) — tell the user this means building out named channels and hours next, not the skill's file name.
5. Model a proposed growth loop as input → value-producing action → output → reinvestment/return to input. State who benefits, why the behavior repeats, the friction or saturation limit, and the evidence needed. A referral label alone is not a loop.
6. Assess economics proportionately: acquisition cost, conversion, retention, gross contribution/cost to serve, payback, and willingness to pay when relevant. Define denominators and time periods. Keep unknown values unknown; do not manufacture lifetime value from thin early data or forecast compounding from an untested loop.
7. Select a small number of bets with assumptions, target cohort, leading/outcome metrics, guardrails, learning cost, owner, and stopping/review rules. Use `pm-discovery` for tests and `pm-objectives` for metric definitions; sequence through `pm-roadmap`.
8. Protect product value and user trust. Do not use deceptive urgency, manipulative consent, involuntary invitations, or spam as growth tactics. Planning does not authorize advertising spend, outreach, publication, pricing changes, or production experiments.

## Output
- Growth objective, current constraint, and evidence status.
- Recommended motion/channel or loop, alternatives, and trade-offs.
- Value/retention logic and economic assumptions.
- Prioritized experiments, metrics/guardrails, and next review decision.

## Teach while working
Explain the relevant difference between acquisition and retained value, or between a funnel and a loop, using the proposed product behavior. Tie growth to the product strategy rather than a separate list of promotional ideas.

## Adaptable prompt
“Identify the most plausible growth constraint for this product using the available evidence. Compare suitable growth motions and loops, connect them to customer value and strategy, and recommend small tests with clear measures and economics. Label untested assumptions and avoid unsupported growth forecasts.”
