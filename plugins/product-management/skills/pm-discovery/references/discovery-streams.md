# Initial and continuous discovery

Adapted from Paweł Huryn, *How to Fix the Double Diamond of Design Thinking?*, June 9, 2024. Source originals are not included; use the bundled guidance and Markdown worksheets.

Use Explore → Define → Ideate → Test with iteration within and between the two diamonds. Choose the starting context:
- Initial discovery starts with a rough market/product idea. Explore segments, needs, alternatives and constraints; define the relevant market and value hypothesis. Compare product concepts, channels, pricing and positioning before testing consequential assumptions.
- Continuous discovery starts with a strategically meaningful outcome or problem. Explore evidence, define specific opportunities and segments, compare solutions, and test relevant value, usability, viability, feasibility and ethical assumptions.

Explain the current question and draft a useful next step rather than insisting on completing every phase. Convergence selects a promising direction based on evidence; it does not reveal one objectively correct answer.

Discovery and delivery can run concurrently with the same cross-functional contributors. Keep release outcomes, analytics, support and interviews feeding back into discovery. A handoff records what evidence supports a bet, unresolved risks, acceptance measures and revisit triggers. Do not use “validated backlog” to imply certainty, require all discovery to finish before delivery, or forbid code-based experiments.

For diagrams use pm-visuals, preferring Lucid. Label the two diamonds Market/Product for initial discovery and Problem Space/Solution Space for continuous discovery. Include learning loops. A third delivery diamond is an optional explanatory variant, not a required process or another approval gate; identify it as a variant and preserve source attribution when adapting figures.

The article criticizes the original Design Council diagram as linear. Treat this as the author's critique of a representation, not a verified claim that current Design Council practice excludes iteration. No full-book reading of Sprint or IDEO materials is implied. Teach the useful distinction between learning what to build and delivering it without presenting one framework as universally correct.
