# Pretotyping lessons related to The Right It

Scope: a practical synthesis from Alberto Savoia's public pretotyping materials and ProductCompass references. The full book **The Right It** is not reproduced here; this is not a chapter extraction or a claim to cover the whole book.

## Apply before expensive delivery when demand is uncertain
Separate testing whether people will engage with an idea from testing whether the implementation works. A prototype can reveal usability or feasibility; a pretotype targets a consequential demand/engagement assumption with the smallest useful representation or service. Choose the method by the question, not the label.

1. Identify the assumption whose failure would most undermine the idea.
2. Turn a vague market-engagement belief into a testable **XYZ hypothesis**: “At least X% of Y will do Z.” Define the target population, meaningful observable behavior, exposure denominator, timeframe, and conditions. The percentage is a proposed threshold, not a discovered fact. Justify it from the decision/economics rather than choosing a flattering number.
3. Narrow to a reachable, relevant initial test group. Explain the limits of generalizing beyond it; a small convenience sample cannot prove the entire market.
4. Choose a minimal test: a disclosed manual/concierge service, simple stand-in experience, or accurately described landing-page interest test, for example. Build only what the hypothesis requires. A click tests a different commitment from repeated use or payment.
5. Collect first-hand behavioral evidence from the actual experiment. Keep opinions, secondary market evidence, expressed interest, costly commitment, and observed repeated behavior distinct. Do not treat enthusiasm as willingness to pay.
6. Predetermine what pass, fail, or inconclusive means. Record who saw the offer, who acted, recruitment bias, costs, and what the result does and does not establish. Revise or stop a weak idea rather than changing the success criterion after the fact.
7. Design truthful, proportionate tests. Do not take payment for an unavailable product without clear terms and authorization; do not hide material limitations or create harmful fake experiences. Publishing, outreach, payment, or production tests require the user's applicable authorization.

## Experiment card
Decision; riskiest assumption; XYZ hypothesis; target group and recruitment; behavior and exposure denominator; minimal test; threshold rationale; duration; cost; disclosure/risks; observed result; limitations; proceed/change/stop decision.

## Teaching example
For an imaginary scheduling tool, “people like the idea” is weak evidence. A disclosed manual scheduling trial can test whether a defined group actually submits scheduling requests and returns to use the service. It does not yet establish software feasibility, acquisition economics, or demand outside that group. Supply no invented conversion target.
