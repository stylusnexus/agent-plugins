# Product risk decisions

Adapted from Paweł Huryn, *How to Manage Risks as a Product Manager*, June 2, 2024. Source originals are not included; use the bundled guidance and Markdown worksheets.

Consider uncertain events or assumptions that could affect the desired outcome. Separate downside threats from upside possibilities. An upside possibility is not necessarily a customer opportunity in an Opportunity Solution Tree.

## Scope risk to the decision
Use value, usability, feasibility and viability, with ethics explicit or under viability. For a new product, make go-to-market exposure visible separately: audience access, promise, channel, purchase, onboarding and launch readiness. Also examine strategy/objective assumptions and team capacity, skills, dependencies and ownership. These are connected lenses, not rigid departmental boundaries; anyone can surface a risk.

For each significant risk record: ID, objective, uncertain condition/event, evidence, consequence, exposure or likelihood if defensible, reversibility, response, owner, trigger and review date. Do not invent probabilities or multiply ordinal scores into false precision. A current known failure is an issue to handle, not merely an uncertain risk.

Select an explicit response: avoid the exposure, reduce it through design or learning, or accept it with rationale and accountable ownership. For upside, identify a proportionate action to improve the chance of benefit or consciously decline action. Acceptance does not override existing authorization, obligations or guardrails.

Prioritize tests by how a result could change the decision, consequences, uncertainty and learning cost. Not every assumption needs an experiment. Reversible, limited-exposure choices can proceed with monitoring when authorized; high-consequence unknowns may justify more evidence. Retain residual risk after testing.

A preorder/landing-page test can support a proposition about commitment in the reached sample. It does not prove total-market demand, delivered value, usability or retention. Sample selection, refunds, timing and the exact purchase conditions matter. The article's hypothetical conversion numbers are not benchmarks or proof for the user's product.

Link strategy risk to outcome measures and revisit triggers, objective risk to alternative bets/capacity, and team risk to pm-teams. Parallel teams are an option for a critical problem only when the potential learning justifies capacity and coordination cost. Do not prescribe duplication by default.

For authorized releases, use bounded exposure, instrumentation, monitoring, rollback/disable and an accountable responder. Early release is a learning opportunity, not permission to expose customers to uncontrolled harm. Distinguish telemetry about usage from evidence of achieved customer outcomes.

## Teaching and output
Work through one real risk together: “What could prevent this outcome, what do we know, and what would change our decision?” Deliver a short risk table and decision, linked to the PRD/roadmap. Do not create a separate bureaucracy for a small reversible change.
