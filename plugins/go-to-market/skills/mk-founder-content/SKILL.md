Content published under a founder's own name — LinkedIn and social posts, blog posts, newsletters, launch stories, "building in public" updates — planned and drafted at a pace a small team can actually sustain.

## When to use this skill

Planning what a founder should post, drafting a specific post, finding a true story for a launch, or building a weekly content system from one real piece of thinking.

## Workflow

1. **Find a real story before drafting anything.** A claim lands harder with a true before/after behind it than an adjective in front of it — see [story-finding.md](references/story-finding.md). Ask "where did something real actually change," not "what should I write about." Every beat has to be checkable: a real message, date, number, or a direct account from the person it happened to. Nothing invented to make the shape fit better, even if it reads better that way. Hard rule for any post: never invent a product capability, a person's stated reason, a result, or "we've talked to X" evidence — check every sentence against a supplied fact and label a single self-reported result as self-reported, not verified (see `marketing-lead/references/principles.md`).

2. **Set the system to the hours the team actually has, not a posting target.** See [content-system.md](references/content-system.md): pick the hours per week first, then write one substantial source piece per cycle and publish it in full on a space the team owns. Cut the week's shorter posts from that one piece (a finding, a how-to, a trade-off, a proof point, a question) rather than starting each one from a blank page. Choose the lowest posting pace that fits the budget and can hold for eight weeks — the right number is the number the team can stick to, not a target borrowed from someone else's schedule.

3. **Match the give-first habit to where the actual audience is.** See [give-first-and-audience-differences.md](references/give-first-and-audience-differences.md): a professional-network audience (LinkedIn) rewards substantial comments on other people's posts before ever mentioning your own; a hobby audience rewards participation in its own forums and Discords (`mk-community`); a developer/open-source audience's real surface is the README, docs, and issue tracker, not a social feed. Confirm which audience this is (`mk-audience`) before picking the routine — running the wrong one is giving in the wrong room.

4. **Post with the platform's actual, current mechanics in mind.** See [feed-mechanics.md](references/feed-mechanics.md) for what each major platform states about its own ranking (dwell time on LinkedIn, in-network vs. out-of-network reach on X, connected vs. recommended content on Facebook, satisfaction signals on YouTube). This is a snapshot from one research pass — re-verify against the live page before relying on it, and ask a platform-specific question rather than applying one universal rule across all of them.

5. **Batch, check, then ask — never post on a plan alone.** Draft the week's posts in one sitting while the source piece is fresh. Fill every slot in the week's plan with a usable topic — where no real fact exists yet for a slot, mark it `MISSING INFORMATION: needs [specific fact] from owner` rather than leaving it blank or inventing one. Check every post for unsourced claims and any name that hasn't been cleared to appear. Show the owner before scheduling anything — a plan is not permission (see `marketing-lead/references/principles.md`).
6. **After posting, show up for the reply.** Reply to comments on the founder's own posts — the give-first habit doesn't end at publish. Where a post ends on a question, its real replies are a live input for the next source piece or short post, not just engagement.
7. **Set a stop rule for the cadence itself.** Pick the one metric this content is meant to move (per `mk-measurement`). After a set number of weeks — state the number up front — with no real movement on that metric, reduce posting cadence and revisit the topic, platform, or audience rather than continuing to post at the same rate on faith.

## Routing to sibling skills

- `mk-community` — for the community-participation half of "building in public" (Discord, forums, open-source repos), as distinct from solo social posting.
- `mk-outreach` — when a piece of content becomes the basis for a targeted pitch to a journalist or podcast host.
- `mk-positioning` — for the underlying message a founder's content should keep reinforcing.
- `mk-measurement` — for tracking reach and engagement honestly, with a real denominator and time window.
- `marketing-lead` — for how this content system fits the team's broader, hours-bounded plan; voice for content under a specific person's name follows that person's own documented voice profile, per `marketing-lead/references/principles.md`.

## Outputs

A filled [weekly content plan](templates/weekly-content-plan.md) built from one source piece. A filled [post-drafting worksheet](templates/post-drafting-worksheet.md) per post, with facts, assumptions, and any illustrative examples clearly labeled. Neither is a publish — both go to the owner for review first.

## Adaptable prompt

"Here's the source piece I want to build this week's content from: [paste or describe it]. We have about [N] hours this week for content. Cut it into [N] short posts for [platform(s)], each with a single point and a real, cited fact or number — no post without one. Flag anything that needs my sign-off before it goes out."
