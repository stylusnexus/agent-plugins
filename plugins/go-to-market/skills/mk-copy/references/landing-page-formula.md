# Landing-page copy formula

Julian Shapiro's public landing-page guidance frames every copy decision as a trade against one working equation: **willingness to act rises with desire, and falls with labor and confusion.** The page structure and the objection-finding question below are adapted from the same guidance, credited here rather than passed off as this plugin's own invention. Source: Julian Shapiro, "Landing Page Copywriting" — https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages, accessed 2026-09-24.

## The trade-off, as a working test
Before shipping any line, ask which side of the equation it moves: does it make the reader want the outcome more, or does it remove a reason to hesitate? A sentence that just "sounds impressive" without doing either isn't earning its place on the page.

## Writing the headline in two passes
1. **Plain value proposition first.** State the problem without the product against the better outcome with it, as a concrete action statement. A stranger who reads only the headline should know exactly what the product does — avoid vague corporate phrasing ("empower your workflow," "unlock your potential").
2. **Add one hook.** Layer in a single bold, specific claim, or preempt the single biggest objection. Resist handling every objection in the headline; pick one deliberately and leave the rest for the body.

## Page structure
Shapiro's guide recommends a specific section order and warns that straying from it tends to confuse the average visitor. Loosely, in his order: identity/nav, then the hero, then proof if there is any real proof to show, then a first ask, then the features (each carrying the doubt it settles), a second ask, and closing links. Treat the order as a starting default worth deviating from once there's a specific reason to, not a fixed rule — the source above has the fuller reasoning if a team wants to follow it more closely.

## Finding the real objection
Shapiro's method for finding objections is to ask real users and non-buyers, directly, what nearly kept them from saying yes — and to build the copy around whatever they actually say back, not around a doubt invented on their behalf. Guessing at objections from imagination is a weaker exercise than answering a real one — this step depends on the audience research a positioning or audience pass should already have collected.

## For a technical or developer audience
Replace adjectives with demonstration: real screenshots, real code, real output. A technical reader's biggest unstated objection is usually "how much friction before I see this work" — a runnable example answers that faster than any sentence can.
