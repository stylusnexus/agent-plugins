# Pre-publish content check

Run this before anything drafted in `mk-copy` goes out. It pulls from public plain-language, accessibility, and truth-in-advertising guidance, plus this plugin's own honesty rules — not a proprietary scoring system, just the real external bars this kind of content has to clear.

## Plain language
Grounded in the U.S. government's own plain-language guidance, written because the Plain Writing Act of 2010 requires public-facing content to be "written for its specific audience" and understandable on a first read. (Source: digital.gov, "Plain language," https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language, accessed 2026-09-24.)

- [ ] A reader unfamiliar with the product can follow the copy on one read, no re-reading required.
- [ ] Every acronym and piece of jargon is defined at first use, or cut.
- [ ] Sentences carry one idea each; long compound sentences are split.
- [ ] Headings and structure let a skimmer find the part relevant to them without reading the whole page.

## Accessibility basics
Grounded in W3C's own accessibility guidance and the WCAG 2.2 success criteria most relevant to written content, not just code. (Source: W3C, "Introduction to Web Accessibility," https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-intro/, accessed 2026-09-24; W3C, "WCAG 2.2," https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/, accessed 2026-09-24.)

- [ ] Every image that carries meaning has real, descriptive alt text — not the filename, not "image."
- [ ] Link text describes the destination ("see the pricing page," not "click here").
- [ ] No meaning is carried by color alone (a status shown only as a colored dot also gets a text label).
- [ ] Heading levels are used for actual structure, not just for visual size.

## Truthful claims
Grounded in the FTC's Endorsement Guides, which require that "an endorsement must reflect the honest opinion of the endorser" and that any connection between an endorser and the marketer "a significant minority of consumers wouldn't expect" be disclosed clearly. The same guides hold that an ad reporting an exceptional result must say what's typical, when there's no proof the exceptional result is normal. (Source: FTC, "FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking," https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking, accessed 2026-09-24.)

- [ ] Every testimonial or review quoted is real and attributable, not composited or invented.
- [ ] Any paid, free-product, or employment relationship behind an endorsement is disclosed.
- [ ] A result presented as typical actually is typical, or the copy says it isn't guaranteed.
- [ ] No manufactured urgency or scarcity language attached to a limit that isn't real.

## This plugin's own rule
- [ ] Every factual claim in the copy has a row in the claim → source table, and nothing ships without one (see [persuasion levers, filtered through honest persuasion](persuasion-levers-honest.md) for the same standard applied to specific tactics).
