# Hobby and enthusiast community participation

Always fetch each specific community's current rules per [platform-rules-protocol.md](platform-rules-protocol.md) before posting anywhere named here.

## Core pattern: participate first, promote rarely
This suite's own working assumption, not a cited study: hobby and enthusiast spaces tend to notice and reward members who show up consistently and help other members before they ever mention their own product, more than a single well-produced promotional post. A useful discipline for a small team: cap marketing-specific effort at a small, sustainable share of total working time, and spend the rest of any community time on genuine participation — answering questions, trying other members' work, giving credit. Consistency across months, sustained at a pace the team can actually keep, is the more defensible bet than a single big push.

## Reciprocity over reach-buying
Trade visibility with peers rather than only asking for shares: comment on and boost other small creators' work, join their launches, and expect the same in return over time. This works because hobby audiences tend to be small and interconnected enough that generosity gets noticed, and reach isn't for sale the way paid ads are.

## Platform mechanics vary — check the current version
This is a general pattern, not a claim about any one platform's current mechanics — fetch and confirm per [platform-rules-protocol.md](platform-rules-protocol.md) before relying on any of it:
- **Marketplace or portfolio sites** (a storefront for creative work, a pattern or asset marketplace) commonly surface work through search, a "popular" or "trending" sort, curated collections, and a creator's own update post to existing followers — verify which of these the specific site actually uses today.
- **Forums and subreddits**: many large hobby subreddits restrict direct self-promotion in their own posted rules, with process-sharing posts (what you're building and why, not "buy my thing") more commonly accepted. Confirm this against the specific subreddit's current rules — it varies by community and changes over time.
- **Discord and similar chat communities**: many have a dedicated self-promo or showcase channel with its own posting-cadence rules in the server's rules channel; posting outside a channel meant for it tends to read as spam regardless of intent. Confirm the specific server's current channel and rules before posting.

## A durable channel no platform can take away
Build a simple owned presence — a small mailing list, a devlog, or a personal site — as insurance against any single platform's algorithm or rules changing. This is slower to build than posting into an existing community, but it's the one channel that survives a platform's policy shift.

## Convention, meetup, and in-person norms
For hobbies with real-world gatherings (conventions, meetups, local groups), the same reciprocity rule applies: show up as a participant, help run something small, or support another maker's table before making an ask. In-person credibility built this way tends to transfer back to the online community.

## Applying it
For a product that supports a hobby rather than being a hobby product itself, the community that matters is usually the dedicated forums, Discord servers, and subreddits where practitioners of that hobby already gather and talk shop — not a marketplace built for selling finished creative work. Content that shares real process (what you're building, what broke, what you learned) fits this audience naturally and pairs with the founder-content system in `mk-founder-content`.
