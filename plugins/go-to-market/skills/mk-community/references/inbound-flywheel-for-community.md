# Running community as a flywheel, not a funnel

A funnel treats community as a one-way pipe: push content in, hope some fraction converts, and stop producing anything the moment you stop pushing. HubSpot's flywheel model describes the alternative — a cycle that keeps turning under its own momentum because each stage feeds the next. HubSpot names three stages: **attract** ("using your expertise to create content and conversations that start meaningful relationships with the right people"), **engage** ("building lasting relationships with people by providing insights and solutions that align with their roadblocks and goals"), and **delight** ("providing an outstanding experience that adds real value, empowers people to reach their goals, and become promoters of your company") (HubSpot, "How the Flywheel Killed HubSpot's Funnel" — https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/our-flywheel).

## Why the wheel keeps spinning

The mechanism that makes this a wheel instead of a funnel is the last stage feeding back into the first: delighted people become promoters, and "more delighted customers means a 'heavier' wheel, and one that produces more energy when spun" — their word of mouth attracts the next round of people without the team having to generate it from scratch each time (same source). HubSpot's own framing draws the contrast directly: "Whereas funnels lose their momentum at the bottom, flywheels leverage their momentum to keep spinning" (same source).

## What slows the wheel down

HubSpot's model also names what works against this: "friction kills flywheels." In their framing this covers both the external kind — drop-offs, churn, anything that makes it harder for a delighted person to act on that delight — and the internal kind, "silos, handoffs, and specialization" that make a team slower to respond (same source). For a small team running a community presence, the internal version is usually the more common failure: one person handles comments, a different person handles the docs a comment points to, and neither knows the other exists.

## Applying it to community work

The three stages map onto the actual work this skill already tracks:
- **Attract** is genuine participation before any promotion — answering questions, helping with someone else's problem, being visibly useful in the space per [hobby community](hobby-community.md) or [open-source community](opensource-community.md).
- **Engage** is the follow-through: replying to every reply, actually fixing what gets reported, crediting contributors by name.
- **Delight** is closing the loop well enough that the person who was helped tells someone else, unprompted — the only part of this cycle that can't be manufactured directly, only earned.

The [community participation log](../templates/community-participation-log.md) is where this shows up in practice: a give:ask ratio that stays high isn't just an honesty check, it's the input that keeps the wheel heavy. A community that gets asked of constantly and given to rarely is friction, in HubSpot's sense — it slows the cycle instead of feeding it. Review the log monthly for whether engagement and any signs of delight (unprompted mentions, people bringing others in, contributors returning) are keeping pace with how much was asked, not just whether posting happened on schedule.
