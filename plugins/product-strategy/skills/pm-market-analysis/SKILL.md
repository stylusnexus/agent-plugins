# Market and Competitive Analysis

Read [shared principles](../product-manager/references/principles.md). Use the framework that informs the decision; do not fill three templates automatically. Ground current claims in primary sources with dates and geography. Preserve uncertainty.

## Choose the lens
- **Porter's Five Forces:** industry structure and the pressures on value capture. Define the industry and boundary before analyzing rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, and supplier power. A substitutes analysis is wider than a list of direct competitors. Identify the mechanism behind each pressure and its implication for where/how to compete. Do not confuse a company's weakness with an industry force or imply industry attractiveness predicts a specific product's success.
- **PESTEL / PESTLE:** political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal external drivers. Define region and horizon. For each relevant factor record evidence, directional change, plausible impact mechanism, uncertainty, and response/monitoring trigger. Do not invent regulatory requirements or treat a speculative future as current fact.
- **SWOT:** strengths and weaknesses internal to the scoped product/business; opportunities and threats external to it. Name comparative evidence. Avoid generic adjectives, duplicate items, and wishful strengths. Convert the important intersections into options: use a strength to pursue an opportunity, reduce a weakness exposed by a threat, or decline a poor-fit opportunity. A quadrant is a synthesis, not a strategy.

## Workflow
1. Establish the decision, scope, segment, geography, timeframe, and available evidence. State the boundary choices explicitly.
2. Gather only decision-relevant evidence. Inspect company/product primary information, user research, reliable market data, and relevant official sources. Separate observations from forecasts and label stale sources. Do not fill gaps with invented market sizes or scores.
3. Build a concise analysis table: factor, evidence/source/date, effect mechanism, magnitude or qualitative significance with rationale, confidence, and implication.
4. Produce the relevant diagram through `pm-visuals`: a five-force relationship diagram, six-factor PESTEL view, or SWOT matrix. Pair it with the evidence table. Unknown is distinct from neutral/low risk.
5. Synthesize the two or three consequences that could alter strategy, capabilities, value proposition, channels, or priorities. Move into the strategy or defensibility work next (internally `pm-strategy` or `pm-strategy-fit`) — explain that to the user as "here's what this changes about where we compete," not by naming the skill. State what would change the assessment.

## Output
- Scope and framework choice, briefly explained.
- Diagram plus evidence-backed analysis.
- Strategic implications and alternatives.
- Unknowns, source limits, and next research or monitoring action.

## Teach while working
Explain why the chosen framework answers this question and what it cannot establish. Use one actual factor to trace evidence → implication → possible choice. Avoid presenting any framework as a forecasting machine.
