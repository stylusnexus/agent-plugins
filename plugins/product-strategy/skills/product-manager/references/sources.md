# Sources

This plugin teaches and applies widely published product-management and strategy methods. It cites the originator or a public primary source for each one; it does not reproduce or paraphrase any single paid newsletter, course, or proprietary deck. Frameworks explain reasoning and inform judgment — none of them prove that a specific product will succeed, and historical examples in any linked source are illustrations, not current market facts.

## Strategy
- Roger Martin, *Playing to Win* choice cascade (winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities, management systems) and the case for treating strategy as an integrated set of choices rather than a plan: [rogermartin.medium.com — The Motivation for Strategy](https://rogermartin.medium.com/the-motivation-for-strategy-d0396049487f); Roger Martin, ["Five Questions to Build a Strategy"](https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/rogermartin/HBR_May26.pdf) (PDF dated 2010-05-26, hosted at Rotman; the document itself does not identify a publication venue).
- Harvard Business School Institute for Strategy and Competitiveness: [Strategy, explained](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/Pages/strategy-explained.aspx) (trade-offs and choosing what not to do) and [Strategic positioning](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/strategic-positioning.aspx) (relative cost versus distinctive value; coherent activity systems rather than being all things to everyone).
- Michael Porter's Five Forces, via HBS ISC: [The Five Forces](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx).
- W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, Blue Ocean Strategy: [Strategy Canvas](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/tools/strategy-canvas/) and the [Four Actions Framework](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/tools/four-actions-framework/) (eliminate/reduce/raise/create). Distinct from any single vendor's canvas template — do not conflate the two.
- PESTLE: [CIPD factsheet](https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/pestle-analysis-factsheet/). SWOT: [CIPD factsheet](https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/swot-analysis-factsheet/).
- Worked strategy examples cited in `pm-strategy`'s [teaching examples](../../pm-strategy/references/teaching-examples.md) and `product-manager`'s [direction reference](direction-reference.md): Cristos Goodrow, ["You know what's cool? A billion hours"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/you-know-whats-cool-billion-hours/) (YouTube Official Blog, 2017-02-27); Jacques Corby-Tuech, ["OKR's, YouTube and the Danger of Unintended Consequences"](https://www.jacquescorbytuech.com/writing/okr-youtube-unintended-consequences) (2020-01-22); Ryan Singer, *Shape Up* (Basecamp/37signals, 2019), free at [basecamp.com/shapeup](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.1-chapter-02); [GitLab Mission handbook page](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/mission/).

## Vision, objectives, and metrics
- John Doerr's OKR writing and the broader OKR literature (originated at Intel by Andy Grove, popularized by Doerr's *Measure What Matters*): [whatmatters.com](https://www.whatmatters.com/), its [free OKR course](https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained), its [origin story](https://www.whatmatters.com/articles/the-origin-story) (Peter Drucker's Management by Objectives, introduced "in the 1950s" per that page — the book itself, *The Practice of Management*, was published in [1954](https://archive.org/details/practiceofmanage00druc) — through Andy Grove at Intel to Doerr), and its [company-wide OKR examples](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/company-wide-okr-ideas-examples) page (background on YouTube's 2012 watch-time switch; the detailed OKR text used above is from Corby-Tuech, cited under Strategy above).
- Rule of 40, a SaaS growth-vs-profitability convention Brad Feld popularized in 2015, citing a late-stage investor: [Feld's own post](https://feld.com/archives/2015/02/rule-40-healthy-saas-company/) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_40).
- North Star Metric framework, popularized in public form by Amplitude: [About the North Star Framework](https://amplitude.com/books/north-star/about-north-star-framework); [Introducing the North Star Playbook](https://amplitude.com/blog/introducing-north-star-playbook). Amplitude's own materials describe the metric as one indicator of customer value, not a full strategy statement. Alistair Croll and Benjamin Yoskovitz, *Lean Analytics* (O'Reilly, 2013; [overview](https://medium.com/lean-stack/lean-analytics-the-one-metric-that-matters-and-other-provocations-fd3006aab17)) is the earlier "One Metric That Matters" framing the North Star concept builds on.
- Itamar Gilad, value delivered versus value captured as two distinct, co-dependent measures (a North Star-style metric for delivered value, a separate top business metric for captured value): [Why Customer Value Is The Most Important Thing](https://itamargilad.com/customer-value/) and [The Three True-North Metrics that Your Product and Business Need](https://itamargilad.com/the-three-true-north-metrics-that-your-product-and-business-need/) at itamargilad.com.

## Discovery and experimentation
- Alberto Savoia, pretotyping and *The Right It*: [pretotyping.org](https://www.pretotyping.org/), its [methodology page](https://www.pretotyping.org/methodology.html) — which states the "X% of Y will do Z" hypothesis framing directly — and [resources page](https://www.pretotyping.org/resources.html); author site [albertosavoia.com](https://www.albertosavoia.com/), including the [pretotyping planning canvas](https://www.albertosavoia.com/uploads/1/4/0/9/14099067/pretotyping_planning_canvas_by_chris_callaghan.pdf) (credited on the canvas itself only to *The Right It*, not to any adapter). This plugin explains the pretotyping concept from these public materials; it is not a full extraction of the book.
- Kano model: [Atlason & Giacalone (*BMC Research Notes*, 2018), "Rapid computation and visualization of data from Kano surveys in R"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6263047/) — paired-question survey design, response categories, and the evaluation matrix used here follow this published methodology. Facilitation guidance, evidence safeguards, and suite routing around it are original.
- Design Council, the Double Diamond: [The Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/) and the wider [Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/) (divergent/convergent Discover–Define–Develop–Deliver phases), plus the Design Council's own [history of the Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/history-of-the-double-diamond/) for its publication date.
- Teresa Torres, continuous discovery and the opportunity solution tree: [Opportunity Solution Trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/) and the [glossary definition](https://www.producttalk.org/glossary-discovery-opportunity-solution-tree/) at Product Talk, plus her story-based interview technique: Teresa Torres, ["Ask Teresa: What Are the Best Customer Interview Questions?"](https://www.producttalk.org/2022/04/best-customer-interview-questions/) (Product Talk, 2022-04-06).
- Jobs-to-be-done framing draws on the general public JTBD literature (Clayton Christensen and collaborators; Bob Moesta and Tony Ulwick's independent JTBD work) rather than any single course.
- Tony Ulwick, Outcome-Driven Innovation: decomposing a customer job into functional job steps and capturing the desired outcomes customers use to judge success at each step: [Outcome-Driven Innovation](https://anthonyulwick.com/outcome-driven-innovation/) at anthonyulwick.com (Strategyn).
- Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur (Strategyzer), the Value Proposition Canvas: mapping customer jobs/pains/gains against a product's pain relievers and gain creators for one segment at a time: [The Value Proposition Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas) at strategyzer.com.
- April Dunford, product positioning as a distinct, testable discipline from messaging and copy: [*Obviously Awesome*](https://www.aprildunford.com/books) at aprildunford.com, and her own boundary between positioning and messaging: [An Introduction to Positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/an-introduction-to-positioning) (2020-05-04).
- Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* ([momtestbook.com](https://www.momtestbook.com/)) — asking about specific past behavior instead of hypothetical future intent in customer interviews.
- Jim Kalbach, alignment diagrams for journey mapping: ["Locating Value with Alignment Diagrams"](https://experiencinginformation.com/2011/04/19/locating-value-with-alignment-diagrams/) (2011-04-19). Nielsen Norman Group: [Journey Mapping 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/) (Sarah Gibbons, 2018-12-09) and [Triangulation: Get Better Research Results by Using Multiple UX Methods](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/triangulation-better-research-results-using-multiple-ux-methods/) (Kathryn Whitenton, 2021-02-21).
- Marty Cagan (SVPG), the four product risks — value, usability, feasibility, business viability: [The Four Big Risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/) (2017-12-04). Wissam Yaacoub, PMP, PMI-RMP, threat risk-response vocabulary (Avoid, Transfer, Mitigate, Accept, Escalate): ["The Know Unknowns: Importance of Project Risk Management (PRM)"](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/chapters/lebanon-chapter/pdf-and-ppt/march-2019-importance-of-project-risk.pdf) (PMI Lebanon Chapter, March 2019 (per URL)). David Hillson, opportunity risk-response vocabulary (Exploit, Share, Enhance, Ignore — four opportunity responses with no counterpart to Escalate): ["Effective Strategies for Exploiting Opportunities"](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/effective-strategies-exploiting-opportunities-7947) (Project Management Institute Annual Seminars & Symposium, 2001-11-01; pmi.org blocks automated fetches — [archived copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20251015183832/https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/effective-strategies-exploiting-opportunities-7947)).
- AI-assisted PM practice: NIST, [AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) (govern/map/measure/manage; NIST AI 100-1, initial release 2023-01-26); its "incorporate trustworthiness considerations" framing is the AI RMF landing page's own description of the Framework's intent, not text from the PDF itself: [nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). Anthropic, [Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) (Claude Docs).

## Roadmapping and prioritization
- Now/Next/Later roadmap format, invented by Janna Bastow and Simon Cast at ProdPad as an alternative to date-based roadmaps: [Why I Invented the Now-Next-Later Roadmap](https://www.prodpad.com/blog/invented-now-next-later-roadmap/) and the [ProdPad glossary entry](https://www.prodpad.com/glossary/now-next-later-roadmap/).
- C. Todd Lombardo, Bruce McCarthy, Evan Ryan and Michael Connors, *Product Roadmaps Relaunched: How to Set Direction while Embracing Uncertainty* (O'Reilly, 2017): [publisher page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/product-roadmaps-relaunched/9781491971710/).
- RICE prioritization (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort), originated at Intercom and published by Intercom's product team as a public scoring method: [RICE: Simple prioritization for product managers](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/) at intercom.com.
- The "As a… I want… so that…" user story template, credited by Mike Cohn to Rachel Davies and Connextra: [Mountain Goat Software, "User Story Template: What It Is and Why It Works So Well"](https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/agile/why-the-three-part-user-story-template-works-so-well). INVEST criteria for user stories, coined by Bill Wake: [INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks](https://xp123.com/invest-in-good-stories-and-smart-tasks/) at xp123.com (2003-08-17). User story mapping, popularized by Jeff Patton in *User Story Mapping*: [User Story Mapping](https://jpattonassociates.com/story-mapping/) at jpattonassociates.com.
- Job stories ("When [situation], I want [motivation], so I can [outcome]"), developed at Intercom by Paul Adams and named by Alan Klement: [Intercom, "How we accidentally invented job stories"](https://www.intercom.com/blog/accidentally-invented-job-stories/).
- Marty Cagan (SVPG), high-integrity commitments as the deliberately rare exception to outcome-based roadmaps: [Team Objectives – Commitments](https://www.svpg.com/team-objectives-commitments/) (2020-03-04).

## Product manager competencies
- Marty Cagan (Silicon Valley Product Group), the four areas of knowledge a product manager must hold directly rather than delegate — customers, data, business, and industry/market: [The Product Manager Contribution](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-manager-contribution/) at svpg.com.

## Teams and operating model
- Team Topologies (Matthew Skelton and Manuel Pais): [Key concepts](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts) at [teamtopologies.com](https://teamtopologies.com/) and the official post on [interaction modes and common misconceptions](https://teamtopologies.com/news-blogs-newsletters/2025/2/21/team-topologies-interaction-modes-breaking-through-common-misconceptions) — the four team types and three interaction modes referenced in the team-design guidance here.
- Daniel Pink, *Drive: The Surprising Truth About What Motivates Us* (2009) — autonomy, mastery, and purpose as the three drivers of sustained motivation: [danpink.com/books/drive](https://www.danpink.com/books/drive/).
- Amy Edmondson, psychological safety — a team's shared belief that it is safe to speak up with ideas, questions, concerns, or mistakes, originating in Amy C. Edmondson, ["Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams,"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2307/2666999) *Administrative Science Quarterly* 44, no. 2 (1999): 350–383, and restated in her later public writing: [amycedmondson.com/psychological-safety](https://amycedmondson.com/psychological-safety/) and Amy Gallo, ["What Is Psychological Safety?"](https://hbr.org/2023/02/what-is-psychological-safety) (*Harvard Business Review*, 2023-02-15), drawing on Edmondson.
- General "empowered product team" principles reflect widely discussed industry practice (outcome ownership, cross-functional teams, continuous discovery) rather than any one proprietary transformation program.

## Business capability modeling
- SAP, [Business Capability Modeling Guidelines](https://help.sap.com/docs/leanix/ea/business-capability-modeling-guidelines?locale=en-US): used only for the general, vendor-neutral definition of a business capability as an ability independent of how it is currently implemented.
- The Open Group, [TOGAF Series Guides](https://www.opengroup.org/togaf/series-guides): general confirmation that structured capability-mapping guidance exists in enterprise architecture practice; no standards-conformance claim is made by this plugin.

Two templates in this plugin are original table/row layouts, written for this plugin and grounded in named public frameworks rather than adapted from any single proprietary template:
- `skills/pm-canvas/templates/product-strategy-canvas.md` — built around Roger Martin and A.G. Both templates' own files state exactly which rows/sections are the cited framework and which are this plugin's own addition (table design, evidence-status labels, and any extra section). Every other reference in this plugin explains a public method in original wording, grounded in the primary or official sources listed above. This whole plugin, including these two templates, is MIT licensed.

## What these sources are for, and their limits
Frameworks here inform reasoning and teaching; they are not proof that a plan will work, a certification, or a substitute for real evidence from the actual product and its users. Where a source discusses named companies or historical figures (revenue, adoption, pricing), treat those as illustrations from the time they were written, not current facts to rely on without checking.
