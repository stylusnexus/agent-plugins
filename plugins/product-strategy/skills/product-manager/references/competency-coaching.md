# Optional competency-informed coaching

Marty Cagan (Silicon Valley Product Group) argues that four areas of knowledge can't be delegated by a product manager — deep knowledge of customers, of the data the product generates, of the business, and of the industry/market it competes in ([The Product Manager Contribution](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-manager-contribution/); see [sources](sources.md#product-manager-competencies)). This plugin uses that as a starting lens, not a rubric — treat it as one useful way to notice what would help, not a certification.

## What each area maps to in this plugin

| Cagan's area | What it looks like day to day | Suite route | A practice artifact worth attempting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers | Talking to real users, tracing a claimed problem back to evidence, running a test whose result would change the decision | pm-discovery | Trace one claimed problem back to actual evidence |
| Data | Defining a metric including its known limitations, distinguishing an output from an outcome | pm-objectives, pm-growth, any connected analytics skill | Define a metric, including its known limitations |
| Business | Naming a strategic choice and what it gives up, sequencing work against capacity and dependencies | pm-vision, pm-strategy, pm-canvas, pm-roadmap | State one strategic choice and what it gives up |
| Industry / market | Explaining the value to one named audience, tracking a real competitive or category shift | pm-value-proposition, competitive-intel work | Explain the value to one named audience |

This plugin also coaches on two things Cagan's four areas don't name directly, because product work needs them too: working with people (who owns a decision, how a team stays healthy — see `pm-teams` and the chief's coaching) and growth (naming a constraint and proposing a small test for it — see `pm-growth`).

## Use it as a menu, not a test

Default to helping with the user's actual work, not quizzing them. Notice when a concept would unblock the current decision, explain it in that moment, draft alongside the user, and back off the explanation once they clearly have it. No one is expected to be strong in every area listed above on a given project — most real PM work leans on two or three at a time.

## If the user asks for a learning plan or a self-assessment

Ask which real decisions they want to get better at, not which framework they want to study in the abstract. Use their own recent work and their own account of how it went to pick one or two areas worth practicing. Rate a skill as unfamiliar, practiced-with-help, or demonstrated-independently only when there is actual evidence for it from their work; never infer competence from someone using the right vocabulary, and never generate a numeric score — there is no validated instrument behind one. Offer a small, concrete exercise, give honest feedback on it, and agree on when to revisit. Structured quizzes or a tutoring mode are opt-in extras, not a gate in front of ordinary help.

## Keep it in proportion

Cagan's framing, like any other, is one person's judgment about what matters, shaped by the context he wrote it in. Do not treat it, or this plugin's mapping of it, as a hiring bar, a certification, a universal career ladder, or a promise about compensation. Route deep analytical, design, or engineering work to a specialist when one is available, and keep synthesis and teaching with the chief PM — a solo builder is not expected to single-handedly staff a full product organization.
