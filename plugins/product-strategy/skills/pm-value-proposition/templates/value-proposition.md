## What this is
A working sheet for one segment and one job, built on two public frameworks:
- **Jobs to Be Done** (Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta): people "hire" products to make progress on a job in their life, with functional, social, and emotional dimensions — not just a feature checklist. Source: [Christensen Institute, "Jobs to Be Done Theory"](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).
- **Outcome-Driven Innovation** (Tony Ulwick, Strategyn): break the job into job steps, then capture the desired outcome statements the job executor uses to judge whether each step is going well. Sources: [Strategyn, "Jobs-to-be-Done Canvas"](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-canvas/); [Strategyn, "Jobs to Be Done Template"](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/).

This sheet is an original row-by-row layout built around those two public frameworks — it is not the Strategyzer Value Proposition Canvas (see [Developing the value proposition](../references/value-design.md) and [sources](../../product-manager/references/sources.md) for that as further reading).

Mark every row with its evidence status:
- **evidence-backed** — grounded in real data or interviews
- **hypothesis** — the team's best current guess, not yet tested
- **accepted** — a committed choice the team is holding itself to, whether or not it is proven

## 1. The job to be done
*What progress is the job executor trying to make? Name the functional job, then the social and emotional dimensions around it — Jobs to Be Done theory treats all three as real, not just the functional one. "People don't simply buy or pick products or services; they pull them into their lives to make progress."*

## 2. Job steps
*Break the job into the steps the job executor actually moves through, independent of any solution — a needs view, not a current-workflow view. Strategyn states that every core functional job breaks down into a universal sequence of eight steps: define, locate, prepare, confirm, execute, monitor, modify, conclude ([Strategyn, "Jobs to Be Done Template"](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/): "Every core functional Job breaks down into a universal sequence of steps a customer moves through to get the Job done. There are eight:"). Treat that as a checklist to draw from, not a requirement to fill every row — use only the steps that actually apply to this job, in the job executor's own order, and don't describe a step as using a particular tool.*

| Job step | What the job executor is trying to accomplish at this step | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

## 3. Desired outcomes per step
*A desired outcome statement is one specific, measurable way the job executor judges whether a step is going well — not a feature, not a solution. Structure: [minimize/maximize] the [metric] to [object of control], [contextual clarifier]. Worked example from Strategyn: "Minimize the time it takes to identify the correct drill bit size for the material being drilled."*

| Job step | Desired outcome statement | Importance (if known) | Current satisfaction (if known) | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Underserved outcomes — important to the job executor but poorly satisfied today — are where opportunity concentrates. Strategyn's own guidance notes this is rarely the "execute" step, since that is usually where the current solution is already competent; look instead at the steps the job executor is working around the solution, or that the solution ignores.

*For each underserved outcome this offering addresses, state the mechanism — how the product actually changes the outcome, not just that it does. Separate the capability from the benefit, and the proof from the promise.*
