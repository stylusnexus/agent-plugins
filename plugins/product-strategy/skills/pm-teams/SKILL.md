# Product Teams and Operating Model

Read [shared principles](../product-manager/references/principles.md). Help organize ownership and collaboration around valuable outcomes. Use the smallest structure suited to the project; a solo builder does not need an enterprise org chart.

## Workflow
1. Establish the strategic context, consequential customer/company problems, current people and responsibilities, constraints, and actual collaboration bottlenecks. Distinguish observed problems from suggested organizational diagnoses.
2. Frame team objectives as significant problems plus clear measures of success through `pm-objectives`. Carry the chosen segment, value proposition, trade-offs, guardrails, and decision rights into the brief. Leave room for the team to discover the solution.
3. Make ownership concrete across discovery, delivery, operation, measurement, and learning. Distinguish accountability from doing every task alone. Identify missing authority or capabilities; do not assign accountability for results the team cannot influence without exposing dependencies.
4. Read [how empowered product teams work](references/empowered-teams.md) selectively. Explain the point relevant to the observed problem and a practical change; do not turn the reference into a mandatory compliance checklist.
5. For multiple human teams, use [Team Topologies guidance](references/team-topologies.md) to examine flow, cognitive load, boundaries, and interactions. Model current versus proposed arrangements separately. Use `pm-capabilities` to map abilities, not departments.
Support autonomy with active coaching, access to context, and help removing obstacles. Trust does not mean leaving people unsupported; accountability does not require controlling every solution.
6. Invite product, design, engineering, operational, and commercial perspectives appropriate to the decision. Make evidence and reasoning visible, allow constructive dissent, and name the decision owner. Collaboration does not require consensus or suppress disagreement.
7. Recommend a small organizational or workflow experiment, with the friction it should reduce, outcomes/guardrails, ownership, and a review trigger. Team changes are recommendations until authorized; do not imply people were consulted or agreed.
8. Use `pm-visuals` for strategic-context or team interaction diagrams, accompanied by the objective brief, dependencies, evidence, and uncertainties. Choose plain labelled shapes rather than copying book artwork.

## Solo builder and AI adaptation
Keep the human accountable for purpose, consequential strategic choices, and commitments. Use agents as bounded analytical/execution support. Treat cross-functional viewpoints as lenses, not fabricated human teams or independent market evidence. Do not equate AI context limits with the full human concept of cognitive load, or assume more agents improve flow. Reduce coordination overhead and preserve explicit handoffs.

## Output
- Problem and strategic context.
- Current ownership, decision rights, dependencies, and evidence of friction.
- Proposed boundaries/interactions and rationale, with a diagram if useful.
- Context-rich team objective or improvement experiment.
- Risks, unknowns, and review measure; one concise teaching point.
