# Team Topologies: bounded application

Based on the authors' public guidance, not a full-book extraction. Use the framework to improve flow and manageable responsibility, not merely rename departments.

The four types are **stream-aligned** (owns a flow of customer value), **enabling** (helps another team develop capability), **complicated-subsystem** (contains specialist complexity), and **platform** (provides an internal product that reduces burdens on consuming teams). Assess cognitive load: what knowledge and responsibilities must a team carry to do its work? Do not prescribe all four types when the organization does not need them. [Official key concepts](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts)

The interaction modes are **collaboration**, **X-as-a-Service**, and **facilitating**. Collaboration should have a defined learning purpose and duration. Service consumption needs clear expectations and boundaries. Facilitation builds another team's capability. An indefinite coordination dependency should remain explicitly unresolved rather than being relabelled successful collaboration. [Official interaction-mode clarification](https://teamtopologies.com/news-blogs-newsletters/2025/2/21/team-topologies-interaction-modes-breaking-through-common-misconceptions)

## Practical workflow
1. Map the actual path from problem to customer value. Record waits, handoffs, rework, and responsibility gaps from evidence.
2. Draw the present teams as they are; use “unclassified” rather than forcing a type. Add ownership, consumers, dependencies, and interaction intent.
3. Examine whether responsibility exceeds the team's capabilities or creates excessive coordination. Ask for evidence from the team; do not infer overload from team size alone.
4. Propose one boundary or interaction change tied to a specific friction. Describe potential disruption, beneficiaries, affected teams, and what must remain coordinated.
5. Test the change and review flow, quality, and team experience. Avoid irreversible reorganizations from a diagram alone.

A useful diagram has team nodes, ownership labels, mode-labelled edges, timeboxes for collaboration/facilitation, unresolved dependencies, and a clear current/proposed distinction. Pair it with a short table of evidence and consequences. For a solo builder, use these questions to reduce handoffs and improve service boundaries; do not invent a staffed organization.
